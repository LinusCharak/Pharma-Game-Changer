What are Platelets?

Platelets (thrombocytes) are blood components that play a central role in clot formation. They gather at sites of vessel injury, adhere to the injured vessel lining and participate in formation of a clot.

Normal platelet level150,000–450,000/µL

What is Thrombocytopenia?

Thrombocytopenia is a condition characterized by an abnormally low platelet count, typically below 150,000/µL, which may impair normal clotting and cause easy bruising or excessive bleeding.

  • Warning signs include easy bruising, small red spots on skin, bleeding gums, nosebleeds and heavy menstrual periods.

How are Platelets Produced?

Platelets are formed in the bone marrow from large precursor cells called megakaryocytes through thrombopoiesis / megakaryopoiesis.

  • Megakaryocytes extend proplatelet projections that fragment into individual platelets.
  • Platelet production is regulated by thrombopoietin, produced in the liver and kidneys.

What are the Causes of Thrombocytopenia?

  • Reduced production: bone-marrow disorders, nutritional deficiencies and effects of chemotherapy/radiation.
  • Increased destruction or consumption: immune disorders, infections and autoimmune conditions.
  • Splenic sequestration: an enlarged spleen can trap platelets.
  • Liver disease and alcohol: the presentation notes reduced platelet-stimulating hormone, splenic trapping and direct marrow effects of alcohol.
  • Pregnancy and certain medicines can also be associated with low platelet counts.

What is Febrile Thrombocytopenia?

Febrile thrombocytopenia is the co-occurrence of fever and a low platelet count.

  • Dengue fever
  • Malaria fever
  • Scrub typhus
  • Septicaemia
  • Enteric fever
  • Leptospirosis
  • Severe Fever with Thrombocytopenia Syndrome (SFTS)

Why do Viral Infections Cause Thrombocytopenia?

Viral infections may cause thrombocytopenia mainly by increasing platelet destruction through immune-mediated responses and by reducing platelet production in the bone marrow.

  • Dengue, Zika and Chikungunya are highlighted as mosquito-borne viral infections associated with thrombocytopenia.

What Happens to Platelets in Dengue?

  • Impaired megakaryopoiesis: dengue can disrupt megakaryocyte proliferation and maturation, reducing platelet production.
  • Platelet sequestration: platelets may become trapped in the liver, spleen and on endothelial surfaces.
  • Increased platelet activation: the virus can trigger platelet activation, aggregation and premature cell death, contributing to decreased platelet counts.
