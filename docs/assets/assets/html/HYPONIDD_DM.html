What is Diabetes?

Diabetes is a chronic condition in which blood glucose remains abnormally high because the body does not produce enough insulin, does not use insulin properly, or both.

  • Insulin helps move glucose from the blood into cells for energy.
  • Long-term uncontrolled hyperglycaemia increases the risk of complications involving the eyes, kidneys, nerves and cardiovascular system.

What is Insulin Resistance?

In Type 2 diabetes, the pancreas usually continues to produce insulin, but body cells become less responsive to it. Glucose therefore cannot enter cells efficiently, while the pancreas may progressively struggle to meet the increased demand.

  • Excess abdominal weight, inactivity and family history are important risk factors.
  • Prediabetes is often an early stage of this metabolic process.

What is Prediabetes?

Prediabetes is a state in which blood glucose is higher than normal but not yet in the diabetic range.

  • Fasting blood glucose: 100–125 mg/dL.
  • HbA1c: 5.7%–6.4%.
  • 2-hour oral glucose tolerance test: 140–199 mg/dL.
  • Lifestyle changes and appropriate management can help prevent or delay progression to Type 2 diabetes.
