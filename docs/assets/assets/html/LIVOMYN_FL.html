What is Fatty Liver Disease?

Fatty liver disease (hepatic steatosis) occurs when excess fat accumulates in liver cells. The presentation defines fatty liver as fat exceeding 5% of liver weight.

  • Commonly associated with obesity, insulin resistance, diabetes and dyslipidaemia.
  • Early disease may have few or no symptoms.
  • Persistent inflammation can progress from steatosis → NASH → fibrosis → cirrhosis.

How Fat Accumulates

  • Excess calories and sugar increase circulating fatty acids.
  • Insulin resistance increases fatty-acid release and hepatic fat production.
  • Excess triglycerides are stored inside hepatocytes.
  • When fat export cannot keep pace, steatosis develops.

Stages of Fatty Liver Disease

  1. Simple Steatosisfat accumulation with little inflammation.
  2. NASHfat accumulation with inflammation and liver-cell injury.
  3. Fibrosischronic inflammation leads to scar-tissue formation.
  4. Cirrhosisextensive scarring with impaired liver function.

Major Risk Factors

  • Obesity / central obesity
  • Type 2 diabetes and insulin resistance
  • High-sugar / high-fat diet
  • Sedentary lifestyle
  • High triglycerides / cholesterol
  • Heavy alcohol use and other individual risk factors

Management of Fatty Liver

  • Weight loss of about 7–10% where appropriate.
  • Reduce sugar, refined carbohydrates and saturated fat.
  • Regular aerobic and resistance exercise.
  • Limit / eliminate alcohol.
  • Manage diabetes, lipids and blood pressure.
  • Monitor liver enzymes and fibrosis risk.
